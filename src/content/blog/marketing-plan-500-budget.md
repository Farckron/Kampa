## What does €500 a month actually buy?

€500 a month buys you two or three channels done properly, not five done badly. That is the whole constraint. At this level you are not buying reach, you are buying a small number of first visits and then trying to make them repeat, which means most of the money should go where you can name the person it reaches.

Here is a split that works for a local service or retail business:

- Paid social, geo-targeted: €200. Four pushes of €50, one a week, radius under 5 km.
- Something physical: €100. Print, sampling, a stand at a local market, a sponsored round at the club down the road.
- Content production: €80. One photo session a month with a student photographer gets you 30 usable images for €60 to €80.
- Loyalty or retention: €80. Print cards, a small discount you actually honour, the cost of the free tenth coffee.
- Held back for testing: €40. Unspent until you have numbers, then thrown at whatever worked.

That is €500 with nothing left for tools, and that is correct. Email under 1,000 subscribers is free on MailerLite or Brevo, and a Google Business Profile is free. If a plan has a €90 software line before a €90 customer acquisition line, it is the wrong plan.

## Which channels make sense at this size?

Three: geo-targeted paid social, your Google Business Profile, and email. They are the only ones where €500 crosses the threshold of being noticeable.

Geo-targeted paid social works because €50 puts a dated, specific offer in front of roughly 8,000 to 12,000 people inside a 3 km radius. That is enough to move a real number of bodies through a door, and you can change the creative next week when it does not.

Google Business Profile works because it costs nothing and it catches people who are already outside with money in their pocket. Most small businesses have a half-finished profile: no menu, no prices, 11 photos, no posts. Fixing it is four hours of work once and about 20 minutes a week after that, and unlike ads it keeps working after the budget stops.

Email works because repeat business is a reminder problem, not a discovery problem. A list of 200 people who already paid you once is worth more than 5,000 strangers, and mailing them costs €0.

## Which channels should you reject, and why?

Reject anything where €500 buys a fraction of the minimum useful dose. Concretely:

- **Paid search.** Not because it is bad, but because in a 1 km catchment there may be only a few hundred relevant searches a month, and your map listing already captures them for free once the profile is fixed. You would be paying €0.40 to €1.20 a click for traffic you can have at €0.
- **A TikTok agency.** Retainers start around €800 to €1,500 a month in most of Europe, which is two to three times your entire budget. Doing it yourself is possible but costs three to five hours a week, and the reach is national when your customers live within walking distance.
- **Influencer posts.** €150 to €250 for one post from a city-wide account, with no way to tell whether it produced a single visit. That is half your month on an unmeasurable bet.
- **Delivery platforms**, if margin matters. A 25% to 30% commission on a €3.20 product is not marketing, it is a permanent price cut.
- **A new website**, unless the current one actively breaks. A €1,200 redesign is two and a half months of budget spent on something almost nobody looks at before deciding to visit you.

## What does a realistic week look like?

Assume four hours a week, because that is what most owners actually have after the business itself. A workable shape:

Week 1 is measurement and setup. Export your last four weeks of sales, write down the number you want to move, claim the Google profile, open the email account. One €50 boost on your best existing photo. Roughly three hours.

Week 2 is assets. The photo session, uploading images, filling in the profile completely with prices and opening hours. Another €50 boost. Roughly four hours.

Week 3 is the physical thing. Order the loyalty cards, print the poster, brief whoever is at the till. Roughly two and a half hours.

Week 4 is the read. Log the numbers against week 1, spend the €40 test budget on whatever moved, write the first email. Roughly three hours.

That is one month, and it is the unit worth planning in: €500 buys enough to see which channel is worth another €500. Then repeat the rhythm, keeping what moved the number and cutting what did not.

## Where do owners waste the first €500?

The waste is nearly always the same five things:

1. Spreading €500 across five channels so that no single one gets past the noise floor.
2. Buying tools before buying customers. A €49 CRM for a list of 80 people.
3. Boosting posts with no offer and no date in them, so there is nothing to respond to.
4. Not writing down the starting number, which makes the whole quarter unevaluable.
5. Stopping in week 5 because nothing happened yet. Most local channels need two full months before the second visit shows up.
