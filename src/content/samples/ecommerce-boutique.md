## Positioning

Not "affordable luxury linen" — everyone is that. This is Baltic linen with a paper trail: flax grown and woven in the region, cut and sewn by one person in Āgenskalns, sold in eleven sizes because European beds are not all 200×200. The buyer is paying €149 for a duvet cover instead of €59 because she can see whose hands touched it, and the whole shop should behave like that claim is true.

## Who we're talking to

Women 32–48 in Germany, the Netherlands, Austria and the Nordics, household income above average, who already own one linen thing and liked it. She measured her duvet before searching, so she types queries like "linen duvet cover 155x220 stonewashed" and bounces off shops that only sell UK/US sizes. She buys homeware in two windows — late August into October, and the first three weeks of January — usually after saving the item somewhere for three to six weeks. She reads the fabric weight (185 gsm vs 240 gsm) and she will email to ask whether it softens. Average order today is €85; she is the reason the €149 duvet cover and the €38 napkin set sell to the same person.

Not the target: anyone hunting a €25 linen tea towel, and anyone who needs it by Friday.

## Channel decisions

**Pinterest.** She saves before she buys, and the gap between save and purchase is exactly the 3–6 week window this business cannot afford to buy ads inside of. Pinterest is the only free channel where a photo shot in August is still sending clicks in March, which makes it the correct place to spend a solo founder's scarce shooting time.

**Email.** With 6 hours a week and a €700 budget, the only asset that compounds is a list you own — one send to 1,400 people costs nothing and is the single lever that turns January's traffic into January's revenue. Everything else in this plan exists partly to feed it.

**Instagram.** Not for reach — for proof. It is where the mill, the cutting table and the customer photos live, and it is the page a Pinterest visitor opens in a second tab before spending €149 with a shop she has never heard of.

**Rejected:**

- **Paid Meta ads** — €700/month with a €85 AOV and no creative testing budget buys about 700 clicks and a CPA that lands above gross margin; you would spend the whole budget learning what the free channels can tell you for nothing.
- **TikTok** — the format rewards volume and a face on camera, and 6 hours a week cannot produce both that and the still photography Pinterest and email actually run on.
- **Marketplaces (Etsy, Amazon Handmade)** — a 6.5%+ take rate on a made-to-order margin, and the customer becomes theirs, not yours, which is the opposite of the list-building goal.
- **Google Shopping** — the size-specific queries convert well but you have no product feed, no reviews, and no time to maintain either this quarter; revisit in Q4 when the catalogue is stable.
- **Influencer fees** — flat-fee posts from mid-tier accounts start around €400 and are a one-night spend; the same money as gifted product buys four creators and keeps the photos.

## How you'll know it's working

**1. Email list: 680 contacts by day 30.** Starting at 262. That means about 105 net new per week — roughly 175 from the site opt-in (3.2% of ~5,500 sessions), 120 from the sizing-card landing page that Pinterest feeds, 90 from the creator wave, 30 from checkout opt-in. Check in the email platform, Sunday evening, one number in a spreadsheet. Milestone: 470 by day 14. Miss it by more than 80 and the problem is the popup offer, not the traffic.

**2. Pinterest outbound clicks: 1,200 in the trailing 30 days by day 30.** Currently 410. Check Pinterest Analytics → Outbound clicks, 30-day window, sorted by pin, every Monday. This is the leading indicator for everything else — if it is under 700 by day 14, the pins are wrong (bad crop, no keyword in the description) and the week 4 rebuild needs to go deeper.

**3. Revenue: €3,800 in month 1, with at least €1,000 attributed to email.** Currently €2,800/month. The path: ~3,900 sessions at 1.15% conversion is 45 orders, at the current €85 average order value that is €3,825. Check the store's analytics month-to-date every Monday alongside the email platform's attributed revenue (last click, 5-day window). If sessions hit target but conversion sits under 1.0%, the fix is product-page copy — sizes and gsm above the fold — not more traffic.
