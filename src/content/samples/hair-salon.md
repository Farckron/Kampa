## Positioning

The salon nobody in Jelgava has to drive to Riga to replace: two chairs, two people who remember your colour formula, and an appointment that starts when it says it will. You are not competing on price with the €15 chains near Rīgas iela, and you are not pretending to be a Riga concept salon. You are the local colourist a working woman books for the next four years, and midweek is when she gets your full attention.

## Who we're talking to

Women aged 28 to 48 who live or work in Jelgava, colour their hair every 6 to 8 weeks, and spend €45 to €75 per visit. Concretely: administrative and lab staff at LBTU, nurses and admin at Jelgava city hospital, city council and Zemgale planning region offices, Evopipes and the surrounding Rubeņi industrial employers, plus teachers at Spīdola and 4th secondary school.

Two things define her. First, she can leave work at 16:00 on a Wednesday far more easily than she can free up a Saturday, but nobody has ever asked her to. Second, she picks a salon the way she picks a dentist: one recommendation from someone she knows, one look at Google reviews, then she books and stays for years. She is not scrolling for hair inspiration. She is looking for proof that you will not ruin her roots.

Who we are not chasing: students wanting a €12 trim, brides wanting a full wedding package, and men wanting a 20-minute cut. All three are real revenue but none of them come back every seven weeks.

## Channel decisions

**Instagram (organic + small local boosts).** This is where a woman checks whether your colour work on dark blonde roots is actually good before she trusts you with hers, and it is the only place where three photos do more convincing than any sentence you could write. A €120/month boost radius of Jelgava plus 10 km is small enough that the same 6,000 women see you repeatedly, which is exactly the repetition a slow, trust-based decision needs.

**Google Business Profile and reviews.** Roughly two thirds of first-time bookings in a town this size start with someone typing "frizieris Jelgavā" and comparing the star ratings on the map, so your review count is the actual storefront. It costs nothing but the 20 seconds it takes to ask a happy client in the chair, and it compounds: reviews you collect in week 3 are still selling in year 3.

**Facebook local groups (Jelgavnieki, Jelgavas mammas, and the Jelgava buy/sell group).** The 30 to 50 age bracket that matches your ICP still lives on Facebook in Latvia, and a recommendation thread in a 20,000-member local group reaches more of the right women in an evening than a month of posting to your own page. The rule is that you show up as a person answering hair questions, never as a business dropping a price list, because the second one gets deleted by moderators and remembered by nobody.

**Rejected:**

- **TikTok.** Your ICP is 28 to 48 and books locally; the reach you would earn is national and 19 years old, and it costs you the video editing time you do not have at 3 hours a week.
- **Paid Google Search.** Jelgava search volume for hairdresser terms is a few hundred a month, you would burn €80 of a €250 budget on clicks your free Google Business listing already wins, and the map pack sits above the ads anyway.
- **LinkedIn.** Nobody has ever booked a root touch-up from LinkedIn, and there is no B2B angle for a two-chair salon.
- **Printed flyers in mailboxes.** €90 per 3,000 drops in Jelgava, no way to tell which visit came from it, and it puts you in the same pile as the pizza coupons.
- **Your own website and blog.** A site you would have to build, write, and maintain, in order to duplicate what a properly filled Google Business Profile already does for free.

That is the 15 recurring clients. The remaining midweek gap is closed by moving existing Saturday regulars, which costs nothing but the ask.

## How you'll know it's working

**1. Tue-Thu chair occupancy: 42% now, 65% by week 12.** Two stylists, six appointment slots each per day, Tuesday to Thursday, is 36 slots a week. You currently fill about 15. Target is 24. Count it every Sunday evening from the appointment book, one number in a notebook, and do not average it across the whole week or you will hide the problem.

**2. Google reviews: 11 now, 40 or more by week 12, rating not below 4.8.** Check the Google Business Profile app on your phone on the first of each month, and check "how people found you" in the same screen: discovery searches should roughly double from around 180 to 350 a month. If reviews stall below 25 by week 6, the ask in the chair is not happening and no budget change will fix that.

**3. New recurring clients: 15 with a second visit inside the 90 days.** A first visit is not a client. Mark every new name in the book with an N and circle it when they come back a second time. Fifteen circles by week 12 is the goal. At €58 average ticket and 7.5 visits a year, those 15 are roughly €6,500 of annual revenue against €750 of spend.
